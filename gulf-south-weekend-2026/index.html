How the estimates work

Trip energy = miles × Wh/mile ÷ 1,000. Battery drop = trip energy ÷ usable battery kWh × 100. The default 60 kWh and 270 Wh/mile are illustrative inputs, not verified specifications or measured consumption of this Tesla.

At each enabled charger, the target is the higher of the base target and the energy needed to reach the next enabled charger/destination with the selected reserve. Metairie and the Saturday-evening top-up use separate targets. A requirement above 100% is flagged as a route gap, not silently treated as possible. An unreachable leg stops that day’s timeline. Skipping a stop removes its dwell; adjacent planning legs remain approximate and are not rerouted.

Charging uses the selected effective average kW below 80%, half that rate from 80–90%, and one quarter above 90%. These are illustrative taper bands, NOT a measured Model 3 curve. Add 3 minutes to plug/unplug plus the selected queue allowance. Meal time overlaps this session. Site peak kW does not imply the car will achieve it.

Driving uses the editable segment minutes plus the selected percentage delay allowance. Saturday adds pickup minutes, parking/approach time, then walking/security time. “Latest departure” targets arrival at the stadium 90 minutes before kickoff. It is a planning bound, not a safe guarantee.

Saturday’s starting battery links to Friday arrival minus the local-driving allowance and overnight loss, unless an observed value is entered. Sunday links to the Saturday-evening charge minus the same local/overnight allowance, or to Saturday arrival when that top-up is disabled. Evening charging is separate from the pregame ETA. Local miles are a coarse allowance, not a city itinerary. Set real morning values before driving.
